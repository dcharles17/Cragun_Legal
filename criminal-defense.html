Understanding Your Rights in Utah Criminal Cases

When you're arrested or charged with a crime in Utah, you have important constitutional rights that protect you throughout the criminal process. Understanding these rights is crucial to protecting yourself.

Your Constitutional Rights

  • Right to Remain Silent: You are not required to answer police questions or make any statements that could incriminate you.
  • Right to an Attorney: You have the right to legal representation at every stage of the criminal process.
  • Right to Due Process: The prosecution must prove your guilt beyond a reasonable doubt.
  • Right to a Speedy Trial: You cannot be held indefinitely without trial.
  • Right Against Illegal Searches: Evidence obtained through illegal searches may be excluded from your case.
  • Right to Confront Witnesses: You can challenge the prosecution's evidence and witnesses against you.

What to Do If You're Arrested

  1. Exercise Your Right to Remain Silent: Politely decline to answer questions beyond providing identification.
  2. Request an Attorney Immediately: Say "I want to speak with an attorney" and do not answer further questions.
  3. Do Not Consent to Searches: You can refuse consent to searches of your person, vehicle, or home.
  4. Remember Details: Try to remember names, badge numbers, and circumstances of your arrest.

The Utah Criminal Defense Process

What to expect when facing criminal charges

1

Arrest & Booking

You're arrested, processed, and may be held pending bail. Contact an attorney immediately to protect your rights.

2

Initial Appearance

Your first court appearance where charges are read, bail is set, and you enter a preliminary plea.

3

Preliminary Hearing

For felonies, a hearing to determine if there's probable cause to proceed to trial. We can challenge evidence here.

4

Discovery & Motions

We review all prosecution evidence, file motions to suppress illegally obtained evidence, and build your defense.

5

Plea Negotiations

We negotiate with prosecutors to seek dismissal, reduced charges, or favorable plea agreements when appropriate.

6

Trial or Resolution

If we can't reach a favorable agreement, we're prepared to take your case to trial and fight for acquittal.
